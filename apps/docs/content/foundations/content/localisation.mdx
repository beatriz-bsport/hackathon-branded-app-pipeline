Localisation is the process of adapting content, design, and functionality to
meet the linguistic, cultural, and technical needs of users in different
markets. It goes beyond translation — localised products feel native and
intuitive in each region.

## Why localisation matters

- **Enhanced user experience** — People engage more with products that feel
  familiar and culturally relevant.
- **Market reach** — Localisation helps bsport reach broader global markets and
  build loyalty.
- **Revenue** — Localised products convert more effectively.

## What localisation involves

- Translation of text and user interfaces
- Adapting design elements (layout, images, colour where culturally relevant)
- Localising currency, date, and time formats
- Adjusting cultural references and idioms
- Ensuring adherence to local regulations (legal and technical)

## Team roles

| Role                     | Responsibility                                                               |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| UX writers               | User-focused, localisation-friendly source copy; consistent tone             |
| Reviewers                | Accurate translations that preserve meaning, tone, and context               |
| Designers and developers | UI that works across languages — flexible layouts, truncation, RTL readiness |

Localisation requires collaboration from the start. UX writers, product
managers, and designers should work with localisation experts early so local
users get a seamless experience.

## Voice across locales

The bsport voice stays consistent in every language — clear, friendly,
collaborative, and professional. Tone may shift by situation or audience, but
voice always:

- Supports the user and helps them complete tasks
- Avoids jargon, buzzwords, and overly technical language
- Uses calm, reassuring, human-sounding phrases
- Builds a relationship instead of talking down
- Feels the same on desktop and mobile, in short and long copy

Per-language rules — addressing (tu/vous), formality, punctuation — live in
[Locale style guides](/foundations/content/locale-guides).
