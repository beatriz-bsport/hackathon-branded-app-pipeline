title: Inclusive language
description: Writing that welcomes every user — disability, gender, race, and instructional copy.

Clear and precise language creates a better experience for everyone. Everyone
interacting with the bsport platform should feel welcome and included.

## Principles

- When talking **to people**, use language that considers others' perspectives
  and lived experiences.
- When talking **about people**, be descriptive only when necessary to the
  context, and use language preferred by the group being described.
- When talking **about things**, be clear and accurate — avoid metaphors and
  idioms that do not translate across cultures or abilities.

For a broader reference, see the
[EGAL Inclusive Terms Glossary](https://www.egalresources.com/inclusive-terms-glossary).

## Disability

Design for all levels of ability. Considering people with disabilities makes
content more accessible and more inclusive.

**To write accessible content:**

- Centre content around people, not their disabilities.
- Be clear and avoid metaphors, euphemisms, and idioms.
- Do not imply that disability is abnormal, less than, or "suffering".
- Avoid words associated with disabilities to describe objects, features, or
  experiences.

### Disable, disabled

Do not label actions or features using "disable" or "disabled" when an
alternative exists — it can imply that being disabled is a negative state.
Prefer **turn off**, **inactive**, or **deactivated**.

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="Access monitoring deactivated." />
  <Dont caption="Access monitoring disabled." />
</DoDont>

Note: `disabled` remains appropriate when referring to the HTML attribute on
form controls.

### Other terms

| Avoid as metaphor     | Use instead                                                                |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Deaf                  | Only for actual deafness; lowercase unless referring to the Deaf community |
| Blind                 | Only for actual blindness                                                  |
| Handicap, handicapped | Be specific — e.g. "person who uses a keyboard-accessible checkout"        |
| Insane, crazy         | Wild, extreme, unexpected                                                  |

## Instructional copy

Avoid implying a task is easy or quick for everyone. Words like **just**,
**only**, **easy**, **simple**, and **quick** can discourage people who
struggle.

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="Complete this 3-question survey to get started." />
  <Dont caption="Take a second to fill out this easy questionnaire." />
  <Do caption="Enter your email address and password." />
  <Dont caption="Just put in your email address to get started." />
</DoDont>

Marketing copy may use these words to communicate product value — product UI
copy should stay neutral.

## Race and culture

Language can exclude or harm people who have been historically marginalised.
Consider the diversity of users' lived experiences and use language free of bias.

- Do not treat perspectives different from yours as exotic or strange.
- Do not use "black" or "dark" to mean bad, or "white" or "light" to mean good.
- Avoid metaphors and idioms — they often have racist origins or poor
  translations.

### Terms to replace

| Avoid                           | Prefer                              |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Black hat / white hat (hacking) | Unethical hacking / ethical hacking |
| Blacklist                       | Denylist, blocklist                 |
| Whitelist                       | Allowlist, permit list              |

## Gender

Gender is part of many people's identity. Stereotypes can make their way into
product copy and leave people feeling excluded or misrepresented.

- Use someone's preferred gender and pronouns when known.
- If unsure, use **they/them** — inclusive, simple, and readable.

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="A member needs to confirm their email before they can access membership benefits." />
  <Dont caption="A member needs to confirm his or her email before they can access membership benefits." />
</DoDont>

### Internationalisation

Many languages lack ungendered options like English **they**. When possible,
write in a way that avoids gendered language — **you** instead of a third-person
pronoun. Prioritise clarity over dogmatic adherence to guidelines.
