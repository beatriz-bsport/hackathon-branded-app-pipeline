bsport's brand personality comes through in voice and tone — what we say and
how we say it.

## Voice

Three principles form the foundation of our voice:

- **Professional yet human** — Maintain professionalism without sounding stiff.
  Avoid jargon or overly technical language.
- **Concise and clear** — Write for scanning first, reading second. Keep copy
  simple so it works for a wide range of user types.
- **Collaborative partner** — Show studio managers that we are on their side.
  Use short, direct, descriptive language that supports and empowers action.

The bsport voice targets a broad set of users — from technically proficient
operators to people who rarely use business software. Content may change for
different audiences, but these core principles stay the same across all five
language locales. Specific style choices differ per language; see
[Locale style guides](/foundations/content/locale-guides).

## Tone

While voice remains consistent, tone changes depending on context and language.

In English, three components shape tone:

- **Informal and conversational** — Use contractions (`don't` over `do not`,
  `won't` over `will not`). Do not overdo it; clarity comes first.
- **Direct in failure states** — Error messages prioritise direct, informative
  language. Say what happened and what to do next.
- **Short CTAs** — Button and link labels stay short and direct. They can be
  slightly descriptive when the action needs context (`Save changes` vs `Save`).

### Tone by context

| Context     | Tone                 | Example                                                |
| ----------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Success     | Calm, confirming     | `Changes saved.`                                       |
| Error       | Direct, actionable   | `Unable to save. Check your connection and try again.` |
| CTA         | Short, imperative    | `Create pack`, `View details`                          |
| Helper text | Neutral, informative | `Shown on member receipts.`                            |

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="Keep error copy factual and actionable — tell people what failed and how to recover." />
  <Do caption="Match contraction level to the surface — toasts can be warmer; legal copy stays formal." />
  <Dont caption="Stack apologies or blame — one clear sentence beats three sympathetic ones." />
  <Dont caption="Use playful tone in destructive flows — cancelling a subscription is not the moment for jokes." />
</DoDont>
