These stylistic rules apply across bsport product copy — buttons, labels,
toasts, empty states, and helper text.

## Active voice

Prefer active voice over passive voice. Active sentences name who did what and
are easier to scan.

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="You logged in successfully." />
  <Dont caption="You have been logged in successfully." />
  <Do caption="There's been a technical issue." />
  <Dont caption="Your data couldn't be loaded due to an invalid request." />
</DoDont>

## Plain language

Write in clear, everyday language. Avoid slang, internal jargon, and
implementation details that studio managers do not need.

- Prefer **help** over **assist**
- Prefer **use** over **utilise**
- Prefer verbs over noun phrases — **book** not **make a booking**
- Keep instructions direct and user-focused

## Positive phrasing

Focus on what people can do rather than what they cannot.

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="Unsubscribe anytime by clicking the link below." />
  <Dont caption="You can't unsubscribe if you don't click the link below." />
</DoDont>

Positive framing works especially well in helper text, onboarding, and
settings descriptions.

## Contractions

Write how people speak. Contractions make copy feel human without sacrificing
clarity.

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="Your pack won't be displayed on the Member Area." />
  <Dont caption="Your pack will not be displayed on the Member Area." />
</DoDont>

<Callout type="warning" title="When to avoid contractions">
  Skip contractions in legal text, compliance notices, and any copy where
  formality signals trust. Locale guides may override this — French product copy
  often stays more formal than English.
</Callout>
